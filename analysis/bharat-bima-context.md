# Bharat Bima — Working Context & Assumptions
### Captured from working sessions, for continuity across future analysis

- **Company age:** ~5 months old as of Sep 2026. No significant existing book of business.
- **Implication for this whole exercise:** Bharat Bima is not defending or unwinding a legacy commission model — it is choosing its distribution model *now*, under a regime that is still in draft (comment deadline 25 Oct 2026, EoM/commission glide path starts FY27-28). This is a structural advantage vs. incumbent brokers who have sunk costs in models the reform penalizes (group-affinity volume, credit-linked bundling, etc.) — Bharat Bima can build natively for the new rules instead of retrofitting.
- **Originally intended core model:** group affinity business (leveraging access to a captive group — institution/association/corporate/bank-linked — for volume). Per Section 3/6/8/10 analysis, this model is structurally squeezed by the reforms from multiple simultaneous angles (see below). Actively being reconsidered as of this session.
- **No significant own-book data exists yet** to benchmark persistency, channel mix, or commission behaviour against the Section 1 outcome-assessment parameters — this is a gap to fill as the business grows, not a current blocker.

### Why "group affinity as core" is structurally exposed under the reform (document-grounded)
1. **Section 8, Principle 6:** "Commission on group insurance products should be lower than that on products for individuals" — hardcoded into Box 4A/4B. E.g., Group Pure Term multi-year life = 5% (IDE) vs Individual Pure Term multi-year = 25% (IDE). Group health (employer/non-employer) first-time = 2.5% (IDE, capped ₹100L) vs Individual health first-time = 15% (IDE).
2. **Section 8, Principle 3:** mandatory/near-mandatory and credit-linked products get nil/near-nil commission — directly hits bank/NBFC-affinity, loan-linked insurance (Box 4A/4B Section E: Motor TP nil, Life pure-term multi-year via lending entity = 2.5% only).
3. **Section 10:** compulsory bundling of insurance with loans/credit by banks/NBFCs is prohibited outright; only a narrow "acceptable package" safe list survives, each with mandatory disclosure/consent conditions — undermines the classic affinity-via-lender model.
4. **Section 9:** premium must flow customer→insurer directly; a verified direct insurer-customer connection is required before issuance — reduces the affinity partner's ability to control/intermediate the customer relationship, which is the core value driver of an access-based model.
5. **Section 8, Principle 12:** "all-inclusive" commission definition — brand-value payments, incentives, non-cash gifts to affinity/channel partners now count toward the capped ceiling; no more structuring extra value around the headline commission.
6. **Section 8, paras 113–115:** related-party payment safeguards — if the affinity partner is a related party (common in bank/NBFC-linked structures), extra Board-level certification and regulator disclosure apply.

### What the reform's own architecture rewards instead (candidates to replace group-affinity as the core model)
- **A. Individual + complex/commercial risk advisory broking** (property, engineering, marine, large commercial risk) — materially higher, more defensible commission bands (Box 4A/4B), less exposed to group-vs-individual compression, less near-term disintermediation risk from MII/digital "pull" platforms (which will commoditize simple products first).
- **B. Persistency-driven individual life/health book** — Section 8 Principle 9 + the renewal-commission escalator (multi-year life, +0.5% every 3 years from year 6, capped at 7%) structurally reward long-term individual relationships over one-time group enrollment volume.
- **C. Rural/small-town/under-served individual business** — Category-I/II commission bonus (+10%/+20% on first-year premium) is a direct, regulation-built incentive; also aligns with Section 1's Objective 2 outcome-assessment parameters (first-mover data advantage — see outcome-assessment-parameters-tracker.md).
- **D. Non-insurance financial product cross-sell** — Section 6 reform #7 permits IDEs/IDPs to distribute non-insurance financial products. Any "access to a group" value Bharat Bima has can be monetized this way without touching insurance commission caps at all.
- **E. Fee-for-advice model** — Section 8 para 99 explicitly floats a future shift to customer-paid fees instead of insurer-paid commission; most viable first for corporate/commercial clients where complexity justifies a fee conversation. Brokers already carry fiduciary duty (unlike corporate agents) — first-mover positioning available here.
- **F. Advisory layer on top of MII/digital platforms** — rather than being disintermediated by Bima Sugam-type "pull" platforms on simple/commoditized products, position as the advisory/claims/risk-assessment layer for customers who still want guidance — monetized via fee or complex-product overlay, not transactional commission on simple products. **NOTE (superseded 26 Sep):** Bharat Bima has explicitly decided not to pursue any MII/Bima Sugam-linked strategy ("we are not going anywhere near the MII option its not for us") — option F should be treated as retired, not an active candidate.

**Status: A, B, C, D, E remain open candidates; F is retired per explicit business decision.** The portfolio that emerged from later analysis (see commercial-lines-thesis.md, tractor-oem-model.md, affinity-model-unbundling-thesis.md) leans on A (commercial/complex lines), D (non-insurance cross-sell, caveated), and a new embedded/API pattern discovered through NBFC and tractor/OEM verticals not originally listed here.
