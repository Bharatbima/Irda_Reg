## Key Implications — What This Adds to the Existing Theses

**1. Life insurance is a genuinely different commission environment than general insurance**, and none of the existing model theses (affinity, tractor/OEM, commercial lines) account for it directly — they are all general-insurance-grounded. Pure Term life commission (51% avg first-year, up to 81% max) is far higher than any general insurance line, while Credit Life (the classic NBFC/bank-bundled product) sits at just 8% avg first-year — a structurally low starting point even before the reform's caps bite. This confirms the affinity-model-unbundling-thesis.md's read that credit-linked life is being squeezed from an already-thin base, not a fat one.

**2. NBFC/bank channel compression is even more extreme in Life than General.** Life Corporate Agent-NBFC channel currently averages 38-42% (Tables 4/5) with individual insurers ranging 13-51% (Table 6) — compare to Box 4B's capped ~2-2.5% for loan-linked life. This is a steeper cut than the general-insurance NBFC compression already logged (16-88% → ~2-5%), reinforcing that the old affinity-via-lender economics are structurally dead across both segments, not just general insurance.

**3. EoM/expense-ratio data (new, previously missing) gives a concrete insurer-selection tool.** Tables A6/A7 (general) and B13/B14 (life) name which anonymized insurer tiers are furthest from the 5-year EoM targets. Small/niche insurers (sub-2.5% market share in both segments) are consistently the ones with 35%+ expense ratios today — these are the insurers structurally most likely to cut commission below the printed cap first, since it's their fastest lever to hit the glide path. Directly actionable for tractor-oem-model.md's "insurer selection is a real lever" point and for commercial-lines-thesis.md's insurer-panel decisions generally — this was previously a qualitative inference, now it has table-level evidence behind it.

**4. Life insurance broker/channel commission (Table 4/5) shows the same "Others" and "Brokers" categories running high (61% and 43% first-year respectively) — worth a caution flag if Bharat Bima ever considers a life-insurance-linked model: these figures suggest life commission structures currently reward volume/channel leverage even more than general insurance does, meaning the compression under the new caps (Box 4B's structure, topping out at 20-30% first-year for long-PPT individual products) will be felt sharply there too.
