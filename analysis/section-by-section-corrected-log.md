## Section 6 — Simplification of Architecture and Scope of Distribution
**No material impact on Bharat Bima.** Point by point:
- **Capital** (₹10L + scaled deposit vs old ₹75L-5cr broker tier): moot — Bharat Bima is capitalised at ₹1cr, well above any relevant threshold either way.
- **Training escalation** (100 hrs vs 15-75 hrs, Class XII floor): no real impact — training compliance is a documentation exercise in practice both before and after this change, not an operational constraint on hiring pace.
- **Open architecture "choice":** brokers are already open architecture by default today — there was never a decision point here.
- **PoSP exclusivity** ("cannot work with multiple IDEs"): unenforceable in practice — a PoSP wanting to work with a second IDE simply registers under a different name/entity.
- **Hospitals as new IDE-eligible entrants:** not a viable target for Bharat Bima.
- **Garages as new IDE-eligible entrants:** not new in practice — garages already function as insurance sellers via existing PoSP arrangements.
- **Non-insurance cross-sell** (reform #7): the one point that survives as a genuine opportunity — the real question is how much focus/resource it pulls from core insurance broking, not a free diversification option.

**Net effect: Section 6 yields nothing new for Bharat Bima beyond the non-insurance cross-sell option**, already logged in bharat-bima-context.md and opportunities-and-risks.md.

## Section 13 — Market Infrastructure Institutions for Insurance (MII)
**Not pursued.** Bima Sugam/MII is not a channel Bharat Bima is building toward — a deliberate business-direction decision, not a document-driven finding. Logged as a non-issue in compliance-cost-benefit-ledger.md.

## Section 14 — Public Insurance Registry (PIR)
No new content — PIR is registry/disclosure infrastructure underneath the whole reform, not a channel choice like Bima Sugam, so it applies regardless of the Section 13 decision. Its operative mechanisms ("Know Your Insurer"/"Know Your Distributor" and large-distributor performance disclosure) are already captured under Section 9/11 and outcome-assessment-parameters-tracker.md.

## Section 15 — Insurance Education and Awareness
**A genuine opportunity, not a null section.** IRDAI has explicitly named policyholder education and transparent disclosure of distributor conduct/performance as a strategic objective it wants distributors to lean into — echoed in the Section 1 outcome-assessment table's parameter on large distributors disclosing customer-facing performance. A young broker with no legacy practices to unwind can get ahead of this cheaply: publish clear product/pricing/performance information voluntarily, run visible customer-education content, and be an early, visible good-conduct example tied to the reform's own stated goals, before it's mandated. See opportunities-and-risks.md and opportunity-watchlist.md.

## Section 16 — Reforms Objectives, Measures, and their Assessment
No new content beyond cross-referencing Sections 6-15. One forward-looking signal worth tracking: IRDAI explicitly states the sector's overall growth/insurance-penetration gap is out of scope of this paper, and that wider reforms are coming, "sequenced appropriately in the near future."
