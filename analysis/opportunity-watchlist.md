# Opportunity Watchlist
### Soft signals and forward-looking flags — not immediate action items, but things to keep an eye on as the business develops or as future IRDAI consultations emerge.

---

### 1. Digital platforms / interoperable public infrastructure / data-driven underwriting (Section 4, para 81)
**Source:** "Digital platforms, interoperable public infrastructure and data-driven underwriting provide an opportunity to reduce transaction costs, improve transparency and enable customers to make informed choices. However, for these benefits to manifest, the regulatory norms and distribution architecture must evolve along with technological progress."

**Why flagged:** This is IRDAI naming, in its own words, the three levers it believes the sector's future runs through — not a rule, a signal of direction. Of the three, **data-driven underwriting** is the most relevant to watch given two things already in motion in this analysis:
- The MPH (Master Policy Handling) opportunity (see affinity-model-unbundling-thesis.md) — portfolio-level risk placement for NBFC master policies is inherently an underwriting/risk-structuring skill. If Bharat Bima builds real data capability here (loss ratios, portfolio risk profiling across NBFC clients), it sits exactly in the lane IRDAI has said it wants to see more of — and could become a differentiator beyond claims service alone.
- MII/PIR (Sections 13-14) are explicitly data infrastructure plays — any future consultation building on this one is likely to lean further into data-sharing/underwriting-data infrastructure. Worth re-reading this flag when/if IRDAI issues the separate PIR-specific consultation paper referenced in Section 14's footnote.

**Status:** watch only, no action required now. Revisit if Bharat Bima's MPH/claims-data capability matures, or if a new IRDAI consultation on data infrastructure appears.

---

### 2. Insurance education and awareness — proactive best-practices/thought-leadership play (Section 15, paras 154-155)
**Source:** Section 15 frames policyholder rights (access to product/pricing/performance info, insurer and distributor conduct performance) and obligations as necessary complements to the reforms, and proposes routing awareness activities through the existing Policyholder Education and Protection Fund (PEPF).

**Why flagged as an active opportunity (not just a signal):** IRDAI has explicitly named transparent disclosure and policyholder education as strategic objectives it wants insurers *and distributors* to lean into — echoed directly in the Section 1 outcome-assessment table's parameter on "large distributors providing information on their customer-facing performance." A 5-month-old broker with no legacy practices to unwind, no back-book of commission arrangements to defend, and nothing to hide can move first and cheaply here: publish clear product/pricing/performance information voluntarily, put out visible customer-education content, position Bharat Bima as an early, visible good-conduct example tied to the reform's own stated language. This connects directly to the proactive compliance positioning in outcome-assessment-parameters-tracker.md — Section 15 gives it a natural home and a regulator-legible narrative ("we were already doing what you're asking the sector to do").

**What this could look like:** a plain-language product/pricing/performance disclosure page or practice; short educational content aimed at customers on rights/how distribution works/how commissions work; positioning this activity explicitly against the reform's own stated transparency and trust objectives when drafting the consultation response (Section 17) — turning "here's what we already do" into part of the submission itself.

**Status:** a genuine opportunity, low cost / high goodwill, worth building out once the three-part portfolio (commercial lines / NBFC-tractor API plays / MPH) build-order question is settled. Ties together with the proactive compliance positioning in outcome-assessment-parameters-tracker.md.
