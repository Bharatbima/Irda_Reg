## Section 6 — Simplification of Architecture and Scope of Distribution
**Verdict: NO MATERIAL IMPACT on Bharat Bima. Corrected after practitioner review — initial read overstated several points.**

Corrections, point by point:
- **Capital (₹10L + scaled deposit vs old ₹75L-5cr broker tier):** moot — Bharat Bima is capitalised at ₹1cr, well above any relevant threshold either way.
- **Training escalation (100 hrs vs 15-75 hrs, Class XII floor):** no real impact — training compliance is a paper/documentation exercise in practice both before and after this change, not an operational constraint on hiring pace.
- **Open architecture "choice":** false framing — brokers are already open architecture by default today. There was never a decision point here.
- **PoSP exclusivity ("cannot work with multiple IDEs"):** unenforceable in practice — a PoSP wanting to work with a second IDE simply registers under a different name/entity. Not a real constraint on network models.
- **Hospitals as new IDE-eligible entrants:** rejected as a target — not viable for Bharat Bima.
- **Garages as new IDE-eligible entrants:** not new in practice — garages already function as insurance sellers via existing PoSP arrangements. The formal IDE category doesn't create fresh addressable opportunity.
- **Non-insurance cross-sell (reform #7):** the one point that survives as a genuine opportunity — but flagged honestly: real question is how much focus/resource it pulls from core insurance broking, not a free diversification option.

**Bottom line: Section 6, once corrected, yields essentially nothing new for Bharat Bima beyond the non-insurance cross-sell option (itself already logged in bharat-bima-context.md, option D).** No further action needed on this section.

## Section 15 — Insurance Education and Awareness
**Verdict: OPPORTUNITY — proactive best-practices/thought-leadership play.** On first pass this looked like pure public-awareness background (policyholder rights/obligations, PEPF-routed campaigns) with no distributor-facing lever. Reassessed: this is a genuine opportunity, not a null section. IRDAI has explicitly named policyholder education and transparent disclosure of distributor conduct/performance as a strategic objective it wants distributors to lean into (also echoed in the Section 1 outcome-assessment table's "large distributors providing information on their customer-facing performance" parameter). A 5-month-old broker with no legacy baggage can get ahead of this cheaply: publish clear product/pricing/performance information voluntarily, run visible customer-education content, be an early, visible good-conduct example tied to the reform's own stated goals — before it's mandated, not after. This is the same "proactively comply, shows us in good light" instinct flagged in outcome-assessment-parameters-tracker.md's Proactive Compliance Angle — Section 15 gives it a natural, low-cost home: a best-practices education/transparency play that costs little, differentiates Bharat Bima from larger incumbents who have more to hide/restructure, and builds regulatory and market goodwill ("brownie points") ahead of enforcement. Logged as an active opportunity in opportunity-watchlist.md.
