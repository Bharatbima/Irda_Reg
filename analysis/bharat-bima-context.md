# Bharat Bima — Working Context

- **Company age:** ~5 months old. No significant existing book of business.
- **Strategic implication:** Bharat Bima is not defending or unwinding a legacy commission model — it is choosing its distribution model now, under a regime still in draft (comment deadline 25 October 2026; EoM/commission glide path starts FY27-28). This is a structural advantage over incumbent brokers who have sunk costs in models the reform penalizes (group-affinity volume, credit-linked bundling) — Bharat Bima can build natively for the new rules instead of retrofitting.
- **Originally planned core model:** group affinity business (leveraging access to a captive group — institution/association/corporate/bank-linked — for volume). This model is structurally squeezed by the reform from multiple angles (below), and has been superseded by the portfolio described in the business model theses.
- **No own-book data exists yet** to benchmark persistency, channel mix, or commission behaviour against the Section 1 outcome-assessment parameters — a gap to fill as the business grows.

### Why "group affinity as core" is structurally exposed under the reform
1. **Section 8, Principle 6:** commission on group insurance products is set lower than on individual products, hardcoded into Box 4A/4B. Group Pure Term multi-year life = 5% (IDE) vs Individual Pure Term multi-year = 25% (IDE). Group health first-time = 2.5% (IDE, capped ₹100L) vs Individual health first-time = 15% (IDE).
2. **Section 8, Principle 3:** mandatory/near-mandatory and credit-linked products get nil/near-nil commission — directly hits bank/NBFC-affinity, loan-linked insurance (Motor TP nil, Life pure-term multi-year via lending entity 2.5% only).
3. **Section 10:** compulsory bundling of insurance with loans/credit by banks/NBFCs is prohibited outright; only a narrow "acceptable package" safe list survives, each with mandatory disclosure/consent conditions — undermines the classic affinity-via-lender model.
4. **Section 9:** premium must flow customer→insurer directly; a verified direct insurer-customer connection is required before issuance — reduces the affinity partner's ability to control/intermediate the customer relationship, the core value driver of an access-based model.
5. **Section 8, Principle 12:** the all-inclusive commission definition sweeps brand-value payments, incentives, and non-cash gifts to affinity/channel partners into the capped ceiling — no more structuring extra value around the headline commission.
6. **Section 8, paras 113-115:** related-party payment safeguards apply extra Board-level certification and regulator disclosure where the affinity partner is a related party, common in bank/NBFC-linked structures.

### What the reform's architecture rewards instead
- **Individual + complex/commercial risk advisory broking** (property, engineering, marine, large commercial risk) — materially higher, more defensible commission bands, less exposed to group-vs-individual compression, less near-term disintermediation risk from MII/digital "pull" platforms (which commoditize simple products first).
- **Persistency-driven individual life/health book** — Section 8 Principle 9 and the renewal-commission escalator (multi-year life, +0.5% every 3 years from year 6, capped at 7%) structurally reward long-term individual relationships over one-time group enrollment volume.
- **Rural/small-town/under-served individual business** — Category-I/II commission bonus (+10%/+20% on first-year premium) is a direct, regulation-built incentive, and aligns with the Section 1 outcome-assessment parameters that reward early rural/small-town presence.
- **Non-insurance financial product cross-sell** — Section 6 permits IDEs/IDPs to distribute non-insurance financial products, letting any "access to a group" value be monetized without touching insurance commission caps.
- **Fee-for-advice model** — Section 8 para 99 floats a future shift to customer-paid fees instead of insurer-paid commission, most viable first for corporate/commercial clients where complexity justifies a fee conversation. Brokers already carry fiduciary duty (unlike corporate agents), giving first-mover positioning here.

Bharat Bima is not pursuing an MII/Bima Sugam-linked strategy of any kind — this is not a channel under consideration for the business.

The current portfolio built from this analysis leans on complex/commercial lines as the durable core, embedded/API distribution (NBFC and tractor/OEM verticals) as fast-scale extensions, and non-insurance cross-sell as a caveated secondary opportunity.
